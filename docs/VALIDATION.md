## 1. What we are validating

Chowkidar is a compliance-risk workspace for export-focused small and mid-sized
manufacturers. The product watches regulatory and supplier-risk streams, turns
them into cited obligations and ranked risks, drafts remediation, and requires a
named human to approve material actions.

The preliminary round needs evidence that the problem is real and that someone
would pay for a solution. The three questions below are the only ones that
matter for that test:

1. **Pain:** Is keeping up with overlapping EU/US/home-country rules and supplier
   risk a recurring, expensive problem for the target buyer?
2. **Workaround:** What do they do today, and what does that cost in time, money,
   missed shipments, or audit findings?
3. **Willingness to pay / try:** Would they run a bounded pilot or sign a
   non-binding letter of intent at a specific price band?

Everything else — feature preferences, UI opinions, model choices — is secondary.

---

## 2. Target segment and screening

**Primary segment**

- Ready-made garment, textile, leather, or light-manufacturing exporter.
- 100–2,000 workers; at least one EU or US buyer; export revenue is material.
- Compliance is owned by one or two people, often alongside finance, HR, or
  operations. There is no dedicated legal team.
- The buyer already sends codes of conduct, audit requests, or traceability
  questionnaires.

**Secondary segment**

- Compliance consultancy or buying-house QA team serving several exporters.
- A single workspace can serve several supplier assessments; this is a possible
  later expansion, not the first pilot.

**Disqualifiers**

- Large enterprise with an in-house legal/compliance department and a purchased
  platform.
- No EU/US buyer and no near-term export plans.
- Purely academic or student project.
- Anyone who cannot describe a specific recent compliance event or near miss.

**Screening questions**

1. What do you export, to which countries, and roughly what share of revenue is
   EU or US?
2. Who owns compliance today, and how much of their week goes to it?
3. In the last 12 months, what regulation, audit, or supplier issue caused the
   most work?
4. What tools do you use now — spreadsheets, email, a consultant, a platform?
5. Would you be willing to review a prototype with one of your real supplier
   lists?

## 3. Interview guide (30–40 minutes)

### Before the call

- Confirm the person's role and that they own or influence compliance work.
- Ask permission to take notes. Do not record without explicit consent.
- Do not describe the product in detail until the problem section is complete.
- Do not ask leading questions such as "wouldn't this save you time?"

### Opening (3 minutes)

> "I'm researching how export manufacturers keep up with regulatory and supplier
> compliance. I'm not selling anything today. I want to understand what actually
> happens in your team. There are no right answers, and I'll keep your name and
> company out of any public material unless you explicitly agree."

### Problem discovery (15 minutes)

1. "Walk me through the last time a regulation or buyer requirement changed.
   What happened first, and who did what?"
2. "Where did you hear about it? How long did it take before someone acted?"
3. "What did you have to produce — a policy update, a supplier letter, a
   traceability file, an audit response?"
4. "What was the cost of getting it wrong, or of being late? Have you had a
   shipment held, a penalty, a failed audit, or a buyer escalation?"
5. "What does the current process cost in hours per month? Who absorbs that
   work?"
6. "Which sources do you trust, and which ones do you ignore because they are
   noisy?"
7. "How do you decide whether a supplier issue is real enough to act on?"

### Workaround and alternatives (8 minutes)

8. "What do you use today — spreadsheets, email, WhatsApp, a consultant, a
   platform? What do you like and hate about it?"
9. "Have you tried a compliance or supplier-risk tool? Why did it stick or not
   stick?"
10. "If you had to build a workaround tomorrow, what would it look like?"

### Concept reaction (8 minutes)

Show the problem/solution framing, not a feature tour. Then ask:

11. "What part of this is most useful to you, and what part is not believable?"
12. "What would have to be true for you to trust a cited risk finding?"
13. "Who else would need to approve a purchase like this?"
14. "What would make you say no?"

### Commercial test (5 minutes)

15. "If this existed today, would you use it for one real supplier list?"
16. "Would you pay for it? Is that a budget you control, or does someone else?"
17. "What price range feels obviously too cheap, fair, and too expensive for a
    small exporter? Per month or per supplier?"
18. "Would you sign a short, non-binding letter of intent to run a pilot? If not,
    what would you need first?"

### Close

- Ask for one referral to another exporter or compliance lead.
- Ask whether they want a follow-up when a pilot is available.
- Confirm what may be quoted publicly. Default is anonymous and aggregate only.

### After the call (within 24 hours)

- Fill the evidence log below.
- Separate **observed facts** from **opinions** and from **our interpretation**.
- Note the strongest exact quote, with permission status.
- Update the hypothesis status: supported, contradicted, or still unknown.

**What counts as evidence**

- A dated note from a real conversation with a named (internally) person.
- A document the person chose to share, with permission.
- A signed non-binding LOI, or a written pilot commitment.
- A real price discussion with a budget owner.

**What does not count**

- A conversation with a friend who is not the buyer.
- "That sounds useful" without a workflow, budget, or next step.
- A survey response with no identity or context.
- A hypothetical answer to "would you pay?"
- Anything generated, inferred, or embellished by an AI system.

## 5. Pricing hypotheses

These are hypotheses to test, not prices in force. The product has no billing.

| Hypothesis | Segment | Offer | Price band to test | What would falsify it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Single exporter, 1 site | One workspace, up to 25 suppliers, 5 seats | $150–$400 / month | Buyer says compliance is already fully covered, or price is above the cost of the current workaround |
| H2 | Single exporter, multi-site | Up to 100 suppliers, 15 seats, report exports | $400–$900 / month | Buyer cannot name a budget owner, or wants per-audit pricing instead |
| H3 | Consultancy / buying house | Multi-client workspace, 10 client workspaces | $900–$2,000 / month | Buyer requires white-label, SSO, or tenant isolation before any pilot |
| H4 | Pilot | 8-week bounded pilot with one supplier list | $0–$1,500 one-time | Buyer will not commit even to a free pilot with a defined success metric |

**Pricing questions to ask**

- "What do you spend today on the workaround — hours, consultant fees, software?"
- "Is this a budget you own, or does finance/procurement decide?"
- "Do you prefer per seat, per supplier, per site, or per audit?"
- "What would make the price obviously fair?"
- "What would make it obviously too expensive?"

**Packaging assumptions to test**

- A compliance lead is the daily user; the owner or CFO approves the purchase.
- Report export and audit trail are must-haves, not upsells.
- Supplier monitoring is more valuable than a document library.
- A free pilot must have a written success metric and a named owner.

## 7. Validation plan and decision rules

| Week | Activity | Output |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Recruit 8–10 exporters and 2 consultancies from the network; screen 6 | Scheduled interviews |
| 1–2 | Run 6–8 interviews using the guide | Evidence log rows |
| 2 | Ask the strongest 3 for a bounded pilot or LOI | 0–3 LOIs |
| 3 | Synthesize pain, workaround, and price evidence | Updated hypotheses |
| 3 | Decide whether the problem is strong enough to keep building | Go / narrow / stop |

**Decision rules**

- **Strong signal:** at least 4 of 8 interviewees describe a recurring, costly
  workflow; at least 2 can name a budget owner; at least 1 signs an LOI or agrees
  to a pilot with a success metric.
- **Weak signal:** polite interest, no budget owner, no recent painful event, or
  price consistently below the cost of the current workaround.
- **Stop or narrow:** the buyer is a large enterprise with an existing platform,
  or the work is done by consultants who will not change tools.

## 9. Privacy and research ethics

- Obtain consent before recording; store notes in a private, access-controlled
  location.
- Do not upload a prospect's real supplier list to the public demo.
- Redact names, emails, phone numbers, and commercial terms from public quotes.
- Do not make claims about a named company's compliance status.
- Delete interview notes on request.
- Keep this file free of personal data; the evidence log uses roles, not names.
